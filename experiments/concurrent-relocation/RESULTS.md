# Results: age field, survivor spaces and concurrent relocation of old space

Built against `SPEC.md`. `../concurrent-marking/swift` was copied into
`swift/` and extended with:

- an age field in the entry header (bits 57 to 59) and two survivor halves
  per worker nursery (eden 3 MiB, 2 x 512 KiB);
- concurrent relocation of old space by the marker thread, using the object
  table's offset word as the single switch point, a second mapping of the
  heap so that a RELOCATING entry still leads to the old body, a
  post-store check on every store, frame pins and permanent pins, hazard
  slots for the one primitive that holds body pointers, and epoch-based
  freeing of evacuated blocks;
- a relocation verifier at every pause.

55 headless tests pass (34 inherited, now run with N = 2 and relocation of
every live block in every cycle in the threaded ones, plus 21 new), also
under ThreadSanitizer (`swift test --sanitize=thread`, clean after the fixes
in section 6). Every test runs with every verifier on.

All numbers come from one `./bench.sh` run. `bench-output.txt` is the report
verbatim; raw lines are in `bench-raw-kernels.txt`,
`bench-raw-scenarios.txt` and `bench-raw-pauses.txt.gz` (one line per pause
and per concurrent cycle); `PARTS=report ./bench.sh` regenerates the report.
Measurements outside the bench (development, the R1 reproduction check) are
marked as such. Machine: Apple M2 Max (8 P + 4 E cores), macOS 27.0.1,
Swift 6.4, no thread pinning, built-in display at 120.00 Hz. Nurseries 4 MiB
everywhere except the extras. Kernels: 5 interleaved runs, medians; pause and
cycle distributions pooled over the 5 runs. Scenarios: 3 runs of 10 s after
1 s of warm-up, median run by missed frames then maximum gap.

Configurations (SPEC.md "Headless matrix"): `stw` stop-the-world full
collections, N = 1 (checksum reference); `adr6` ADR 0006 (concurrent
marking, N = 1, no relocation, post-store check compiled in); `n2`, `n4` age
N = 2, 4; `r25`, `r50`, `r75` N = 2 and relocation of snapshot blocks below
25, 50, 75 percent live; `ragg` N = 2, relocation of every block with live
objects, cycles back to back (the conflict-maximising configuration);
`nops` the `-DNO_POST_STORE` build in the `adr6` configuration.

## Answer

- **Age field under the allocation storm: it helps less than hoped with 4
  MiB nurseries, a lot with 16 MiB.** With 8 workers on `alloc` the promotion
  rate falls from 1490 MB/s (N = 1) to 1206 MB/s (N = 2) and 1202 MB/s (N =
  4), and fallbacks from 64 to 42 and 48 in 5 runs. Survivor overflow is
  almost all of what is still promoted (554 of 573 MB at N = 2): `alloc`'s
  live tree at a scavenge is about 3 MB per worker, six times a 512 KiB
  survivor half. Bigger survivor halves inside the same 4 MiB nursery do not
  help (eden shrinks; 86 fallbacks with 1.5 MiB halves). A 16 MiB nursery
  does: 554 MB/s at N = 1, **78 MB/s at N = 2**, 0 fallbacks either way, at
  the cost of a scavenge p50 of 4.7 ms (3.2 ms at 4 MiB, N = 1). In the app
  the storm (R1, ADR 0006) missed 15 frames with 100 fallbacks; with N = 2
  (R2) **0 missed frames and 44 fallbacks** (runs: 3, 0, 0).
- **Cost of copying survivors twice:** scavenge pauses get a longer tail,
  not a longer median: `alloc` 8 workers p50 3.2 -> 3.1 ms, p99 4.7 -> 5.5
  ms; `survivors30` 8 workers p50 1.6 -> 2.3 ms, p99 2.3 -> 4.9 ms. The
  initial mark now scans the survivor spaces as roots: 1.2 ms p50 with 8
  workers on `alloc`, 0.05 to 0.15 ms with one, added to the initial-mark
  pause.
- **Concurrent relocation works.** 45 runs per kernel and worker count
  across 9 configurations, every checksum equal to stop-the-world,
  including `oldheap` and `fragment` under `ragg`, where the relocator moved
  130 GB (`oldheap512`, one worker, 5 runs) and every live block of the heap
  was copied in every cycle while the mutators rewired it. The verifiers
  (mark sets, relocation at every pause) found nothing outside tests that
  disabled a rule on purpose.
- **The post-store check costs nothing on L1-hot stores and 5 to 13 percent
  on cache-missing ones.** `storeheavy` (512 million SmallInteger stores):
  1.010 / 1.019 (O build, 1 / 8 workers), 1.000 / 1.023 aligned; `ivars`,
  `sends`, `fib`, `arith` within 0.98 to 1.034 in the plain build and 0.98 to
  1.012 aligned. `oldheap`
  (random pointer stores into a large heap): 1.10 to 1.13 with one worker,
  1.05 to 1.07 with eight. The `ldar` after the `stlr` waits for the store
  to complete; when the store misses, that is a memory latency per store. A
  first version cost 58 percent on `storeheavy` through register pressure,
  fixed (section 3).
- **Conflicts are rare.** Under `ragg` on `fragment` (stores into kept
  objects that are being moved, every cycle) 53 stores were redone and 107
  objects skipped for a hazard in 5 one-worker runs that moved 40 million
  objects; on `oldheap` 4 to 12 redone stores per 5 runs. Mutator spins
  while RELOCATING: at most 0.009 ms. The main thread never spun.
- **Footprint:** `fragment` (5 percent of the nodes kept, scattered) with one
  worker ends at 230 MB of old space without relocation and 87 MB with it
  at any threshold (one cycle, 37 MB moved; live 12 to 16 MB); with
  relocation in every cycle (`ragg`) old space never exceeds 17 MB. In the
  app (R5, R5n) old space without relocation grows to 392 MB in 10 s; with
  relocation it saws between 78 and 152 MB (live 47 to 58 MB).
- **120 Hz:** under the storm, R2 (N = 2) 0, R3 (N = 2 plus relocation) 1,
  R6 (R3 plus main-thread allocation) 4 missed frames, against R1's 15.
  Under aggressive relocation of a mutating 256 MiB heap (R4: 10 GB moved in
  10 s, every live block every cycle) **0 missed frames in all three runs**,
  worst run-loop iteration 5.1 ms with 0.23 ms of VM work and no GC wait.
  R5/R5n (fragment) miss 10 to 16 frames with or without relocation; the
  stalls are 6 to 22 ms scavenges of the fragment ring, not relocation.

- **Relocation at 25 to 75 percent** costs about what N = 2 costs: little is
  sparse in these kernels except `fragment`. `ragg` (relocate everything,
  every cycle) costs 5 to 14 percent of wall time on the oldheap kernels.
- **Peak RSS**: `ragg` lowers it where garbage is scattered (`fragment` one
  worker 68 against 279 MB, `survivors30` one worker 33 against 87) and
  raises it where it relocates whole live heaps (`oldheap512` one worker 940

## 6. Pin and hazard inventory

Every raw body pointer the interpreter or a primitive holds, read from
`Interp.swift`, `Process.swift`, `External.swift`, `Image.swift` and the
collector:

| holder | points into | held across | handling | needed for soundness here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Regs.pc`, `Worker.pc`, each frame's saved pc (`hdr[1]`), Process `pPc` | bytecode object (ByteArray) | instructions, sends, parks, slices | pinned for good at image build | yes |
| `Regs.lits` | literal Array | instructions; re-derived from the method at every return and resume | pinned for good | yes |
| receiver in `push_ivar` / `store_ivar` | receiver body | one instruction (entry load, then slot access) | none: no safepoint inside an instruction; stores have the post-store check | no |
| `at:`, `at:put:`, bytes `at:` | receiver body | one primitive call, one access | as above | no |
| `replaceFrom:to:with:startingAt:` (`arrayCopy`, new) | source and destination bodies | a loop over the range | hazard slots 0 and 1 | yes (test shows the lost store without it) |
| `ioResult:` | a fresh ByteArray | one primitive, no safepoint | young, never relocated | no |
| image loader `bytesPtr` | fresh bytecode objects | boot, one thread | before any relocation | no |
| `buildBottomFrame` pPc | bytecode object | stored into the Process | pinned for good | yes |
| scavenge, marker, sweeper, verifiers | any body | inside a pause, or in phases that never overlap relocation | none | no |
| Process stacks, frames | native memory | - | not in the heap | - |

**Re-derivation, verified by reading the resync code.** `resumeCurrent`
rebuilds `Regs` from the Process's saved `pSp`, `pFp`, `pHdr` (the native
stack) and `pPc` (into a pinned bytecode object), and re-derives `lits` from
`hdr[0]`, the method oop, through its entry. `doReturn` re-derives `lits` from
the caller's method at every return. Frame slots (receiver, arguments,
temporaries, operand stack) hold oops only; `push_ivar` and `store_ivar` load
the receiver's entry at every execution. So a parked Process needs no pin,
and in this interpreter neither does the running frame: the receiver body is
never cached. The frame pin DESIGN.md asks for is implemented and counted
but nothing load-bearing depends on it. The five cached pointers the earlier
variants had (pc, literal frame, receiver body, ...) are down to two here, both
into objects pinned for good.

**Primitives that needed a hazard slot: 1** (the new `arrayCopy`; none of the
28 existing primitive numbers).

## 7. What the verifier and the tests caught

**The verifiers** ran in every test (mark sets at every final mark, the
scavenge verifier at every scavenge, the relocation verifier at every
pause), in two threaded kernels under `ragg`-like settings (`oldheap4`,
`fragment400000`, 4 workers) and in the R3 and R4 verifier runs. Outside
tests that disable a rule on purpose they found nothing in the final code.
The relocation verifier's own test frees a block that still holds live
objects and expects failures; it reports them.

**The tests**, each written to fail without the rule it covers (the
"without" variants are run and pass by observing the failure):

1. `theAgeFieldPromotesAtNAndNotBefore` (N = 3: young age 1, age 2, then
   old), `nOfOnePromotesOnFirstSurvival`, `aFullCollectionPromotesTheSurvivors`.
2. `survivorOverflowPromotesEarly`: 16 KiB halves, a 48 KB list: part
   survives, the rest is promoted and counted as overflow; the list is
   intact across the split.
3. `anOldHolderOfASurvivorStaysRemembered`,
   `aPromotedHolderOfASurvivorIsRemembered`: the remembered set carried
   across scavenges (the verifier fails without it).
4. `survivorsScannedAsRootsKeepTheTargetOfADeadSurvivor` /
   `withoutTheSurvivorRootScanTheVerifierReportsTheLoss`: the previous
   experiment's lost-object sequence with the young object surviving the
   initial-mark scavenge in a survivor space. Without the survivor-root scan
   the old list is missed. This is why survivors are roots at the initial
   mark (SPEC.md "Snapshot soundness with survivors").
5. `aStoreDuringRelocatingLandsInTheNewBody` /
   `withoutThePostStoreCheckTheStoreIsLost`: an object held in RELOCATING

6. `aFramePinnedObjectIsNotMoved` / `withoutThePinRuleTheFrameObjectIsMoved`:

7. `aHazardedObjectIsSkipped` / `withoutTheHazardCheckThePrimitivesStoreIsLost`:

8. `aBlockIsNotFreedBeforeEveryWorkersEpochPasses` /

9. `relocationEvacuatesSparseBlocksAndKeepsTheObjects`,

**During development:**

- **ThreadSanitizer found a Swift access race in the marker loop**: the
  relocator's "retry freeing every millisecond" read two fields of its
  `Marker` record while it waited outside the VM, and a stopper draining
  marker stacks in the final mark held a modifying access to the same
  record. Fixed by reading the marker id once and keeping the retry time in
  a local. It also reported three races in the new tests' own polling
  (plain reads of worker counters from the test thread), fixed with an
  atomic flag and Process state read inside the VM.
- **The post-store check's register pressure** (section 4).
- **The block live-byte accumulation** cost R1 a third more fallbacks while
  doing nothing (relocation off); now only done when relocating.
- **The first `fragment` did not fragment.** Keepers stored into a keep
  store while young were copied together by the scavenge (it scans
  remembered holders' referents in order), so old space came out as dense
  keeper blocks and empty ring blocks, and relocation had nothing to do.
  Keepers are now taken from the ring when evicted, already old. A copying
  nursery compacts what it promotes together; scattered survivors in old
  space come from objects that die after promotion around ones that do not.
- **A latent bug from the previous experiment**: the entry sweep exchanged
  a whole `surv` word with 0 per log record, which loses a survivor bit
  when a word appears in two records of one cycle. With survivor logs that
  is common; it is now per bit.
- **A cycle record held a Swift Array** (per-cycle relocation counters) in

  review, replaced by a tuple.
- **A GCD timer test** (`delayWaitUsesAGCDTimerAndPendingRoot`) needed its

  relocation tests running in parallel its callback waited up to 25 s for a
  GCD thread (alone: 45 ms). Two new tests that first used GCD threads were
  moved to pthreads for the same reason.

## 8. Honest notes

**What was hard:**

1. **Making RELOCATING readable.** The offset word must change state with
   one atomic write, yet a mutator reading the body during the copy must
   reach the intact old body without a mask or a branch. Mapping the heap
   twice (one memory object, mapped at `base` and `base + 2^32`) lets the
   state live in bit 32 of the offset: the address simply lands in the
   alias. It costs address space, not memory, and mutator reads stay a
   plain load. ThreadSanitizer cannot see that an access through the alias
   and one through the base touch the same byte; that part of the protocol
   is covered by the tests and the ordering argument, not by the tool.
2. **The age field broke three things that "nothing young after a
   scavenge" had made free**: the claim (the young bit no longer means
   unclaimed; parity tags fix it), the remembered set (holders must carry
   over) and the snapshot (survivors must be roots). Each has a test.
3. **The interpreter is still sensitive to call shape** (section 4).

**What broke or disappointed:**

- The age field with 4 MiB nurseries does not tame the storm: survivor
  overflow. It takes a bigger nursery, and that lengthens scavenges.
- The `fragment` scenarios drop frames from scavenges, not relocation.
- This VM is a few percent slower than the previous one under the storm at
  N = 1 (section 2, R1), unattributed beyond "the new scavenge and marking
  bookkeeping".
- The post-store check is not free on cache-missing stores (5 to 13
  percent on `oldheap`).

**What a real system needs next:**

- **A cheaper store check.** The seq_cst store-then-load is what costs on
  misses. Alternatives to measure: a pre-store check of the offset word
  (relaxed) combined with a handshake (the relocator flips a batch of
  objects to RELOCATING, waits for every worker's epoch, then copies), which
  needs no store-load fence; or relocating only objects not written
  recently (a card or a write-epoch per block).
- **Concurrent root scanning through the scheduler.** Frame pins and the
  survivor-root scan are both done in pauses; with many Processes or large
  survivor spaces they grow. The survivor scan already costs 1.2 ms with 8
  workers.
- **Concurrent young collection** or at least parallel survivor-root
  scanning: scavenges, not old-space work, are now the frame-droppers
  (R5, and the storm's 3 to 5 ms pauses).
- **Large objects**: everything up to 32 KiB still goes through the
  nursery, and relocation copies them like anything else.
- **Sizing**: nursery size decides whether the age field works; survivor
  space should probably adapt to observed survival.
- **Relocation policy**: copy-on-sparse is enough to stop scattered garbage
  from pinning memory (R5: 92 against 392 MB); `ragg` shows the mechanism
  can move a whole 256 MiB heap every cycle without frame drops, at 5 to 14
  percent mutator cost, so policy can be decided on footprint alone.

## 9. Recommendation input

On this machine, an age field with per-worker survivor spaces cut the
allocation storm's promotion rate from 1490 to 1206 MB/s (N = 2) and 1202
MB/s (N = 4) with 4 MiB nurseries, and its stop-the-world fallbacks from 64
to 42 and 48 in 5 headless runs, survivor overflow being most of what was
still promoted; with 16 MiB nurseries N = 2 promoted 78 MB/s and nothing
fell back, at a scavenge p50 of 4.7 ms. In the app the storm went from 15
missed frames and 100 fallbacks (N = 1) to 0 missed and 44 (N = 2). Copying
survivors lengthened scavenge tails (p99 up to 4.9 ms against 2.3 ms on
`survivors30`) and added a survivor-root scan of up to 1.3 ms to the
initial mark. Concurrent relocation by the marker thread, with the object
table's offset word as the only switch and a post-store check on every
store, kept every checksum equal to stop-the-world across 45 runs per
kernel, including relocation of every live block in every cycle while the
heap was rewired; conflicts were rare (at most 53 redone stores and 107
hazard skips per 5 runs, spins under 0.01 ms, none on the main thread). The
post-store check costs nothing measurable on stores that hit L1 and 5 to 13
percent on a pointer-rewiring kernel whose stores miss. Relocation
reclaimed scattered garbage: `fragment` ended at 87 MB of old space instead
of 230 MB with one cycle, and at 17 MB with relocation in every cycle; in
the app 92 MB instead of 392 MB. The main thread held 120 Hz under
aggressive relocation of a mutating 256 MiB heap (R4: 0 missed frames in 3
runs, 10 GB moved per 10 s), and missed 0 to 4 frames under the storm with
N = 2 with or without relocation. Caveats: one machine, a Mac; synthetic
kernels; fixed nursery and survivor sizes; this VM is a few percent slower
than the previous one under the storm at N = 1, which moves fallback counts
noticeably; the relocator runs at QoS utility; the alias-mapping trick has
not been tried on iOS; ThreadSanitizer cannot see accesses through the
alias.

## Deviations from DESIGN.md

- **Survivors are roots at the initial mark**: DESIGN.md's snapshot rule
  ("nothing young") cannot hold with survivors. The adjustment and the test
  that fails without it are in SPEC.md and section 7 (test 4). Promoting all
  survivors at the initial-mark scavenge was the alternative; not chosen
  because it reintroduces the promotion the age field removes.
- **RELOCATING is the old offset plus 2^32 over a second mapping of the
  heap**, not a tag bit that every read would mask. DESIGN.md left the
  encoding open; this keeps reads barrier-free as it requires.
- **Store and load are seq_cst, not release/acquire.** DESIGN.md suggests a
  store-release then an acquire load; that pair may be reordered
  (store-load), so the ordering argument needs seq_cst on both, plus a
  seq_cst fence after the relocator's CAS (SPEC.md "Ordering argument").
  On arm64 this is `stlr` + `ldar`, which is what was measured.
- **Relocation runs before the sweep**, in the sweeping phase, so every
  entry it touches is a stable snapshot entry; evacuated blocks are freed
  by the relocator, not by the apply.
- **Frame pins are implemented but not load-bearing** in this interpreter
  (inventory, section 6); the receiver body is never cached.
- **One new primitive (`arrayCopy`)** was added so the hazard protocol has a
  user; no existing primitive needed it. `fragment` calls it.
- **`fragment`**: 256 MiB soft limit headless (so the non-relocating run
  fits), footprint sampled every 100 ms headless (a run is shorter than a
  second; the per-second curve comes from R5/R5n). In the app it is paced
  (500 iterations per 10 ms per worker), with a 512 MiB soft limit and
  cycles from 30 percent so that they start inside the window.
- **R5 is run twice** (R5 with relocation, R5n without).
- **R4 forces cycles back to back** (`KAJO_CM_FORCE`) as "relocation forced
  aggressive", which also keeps the SATB barrier on.
- **The scenario verifier runs** use the mark-set and relocation verifiers;
  the scavenge verifier (which builds a Swift Set of every young object) is
  left to the tests.
- **Extras** added: survivor and nursery sizes for `alloc`.
- **R1 does not reproduce C2's numbers exactly** (15 against 7 missed
  frames, 100 against 87 fallbacks; section 2).
