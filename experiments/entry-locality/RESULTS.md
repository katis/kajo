# Results: entry locality, and what the table costs the marker

Built against `DESIGN.md` and `SPEC.md`. Everything reproduces with
`./bench.sh` (Mac) and `SCRATCH=<dir> ./bench-device.sh` (phone); every
table below is generated by `report.py` from the raw lines in `results/`
(all tables together: `bench-output.txt`). Phone lines carry the launch time,
thermal state at start and end, battery, `phys_footprint` and its peak, and
(from the Part 3 runs on) CPU telemetry from `proc_pid_rusage`: CPU time,
share of it on performance cores, average P- and E-core clock, IPC.

**Setup.** iPhone 17 Pro (iPhone18,1, 2 P + 4 E cores), iOS 27.0.1, on the
cable, battery full, Low Power Mode off, app built `-O` whole module (Xcode
27.1), automatic signing, team `BDN6M642M2`, one configuration per launch.
Apple M2 Max (8 P + 4 E, 96 GB), macOS 27.0.1, Swift 6.4, `-O`. VM workers
run at **user-initiated QoS** on both machines (see "Deviations": the
earlier experiments' workers ran at `default`). Medians of 5 interleaved runs
for Part 3, 3 for Part 1 and the supplementary checks. The phone stayed at
`nominal` or `fair` throughout (never `serious`); cool-downs to nominal ran
before each matrix and between Part 1 and Part 3 runs. **The Mac was in
interactive use during the runs** (Firefox with video and GPU at 30 to 40
percent CPU, an editor, WindowServer; load average 8 to 11), which made its
16 to 64 MB region and some VM rows noisy (C/A 0.80 to 1.18 where C is A by
construction). Mac ratios between variants run back to back are usable;
single Mac rows are not precise to better than about 15 percent.

## Answers

**1. What is the phone's memory hierarchy doing? Its caches and
translation do not explain 7x; a power state does, mostly.** Part 1 found
the same translation reach on both machines (L1 TLB 2 to 4 MB on the phone, 4 to 8 MB
on the Mac; L2 TLB 48 MB on both, 3072 pages of 16 KiB) and a page walk that
costs 12 to 14 ns past it on both. A double chase costs 1.1 to 2.0 times a
single chase on the phone (single thread) and 1.8 to 2.0 on the Mac past
the caches; entries in the node's page (`dlocal`) cost the same as entries
elsewhere (`dsep`): 0.92 to 1.11 on the phone single-threaded past the
caches (one outlier at 1.47), 0.99 to 1.07 at six threads. **There is no knee between
50 and 100 MB that explains 7x.** What the data do show: on the phone a
single latency-bound thread on an otherwise idle P cluster runs in one of
two regimes from launch to launch, and the slow one makes every dependent
miss cost 2.5 to 4 times more. The same chase at 256 MB took 91 to 218 ns
per hop across launches; `walk21` on B took 115 to 151 or 408 to 465 ns
per hop;
`walk` on direct pointers after a compaction took 63 to 319 ns per hop
(three launches of five slow at each size) against 52 to 95 fresh. The P
cores ran at 3.5 to 4.15 GHz with 92 to 99.8 percent of CPU time on them in
every one of these runs (the lower shares at 2^20, where the build
dominates), so it is not core placement or core clock; worker QoS (default against user-initiated) changed
nothing. A companion thread on the other P core that **only spins** (no
memory traffic) removes the slow regime: B 287 to 181, L 285 to 164, A 141
to 81 ns per hop on `walk22`, B/A 2.24, L/A 2.03, the Mac's ratio. The
table's walks fall into the slow regime much more often (B 4 of 8 launches
at 2^21, L 8 of 8; A 0 of 8) because they are more latency-bound (IPC 1.1 to
1.7 against 3.5 to 4.8 for A at 2^21 to 2^23), and that is what turned a 2x into 4 to 7x. The
mechanism behind the regime (cluster uncore or fabric clock, or something
else) cannot be named without performance counters, which were not read.

**2. Does entry locality close the gap? No.** L puts every entry in the
16 KiB page of its body (99.95 percent of entries at allocation; the rest
are the large `Big3` arrays) and costs the same per hop as B: L/B 0.96 to
1.09 on the phone, 0.92 to 1.11 on the Mac, 0.91 on the phone with the
cluster held awake. The microbenchmark agrees (`dlocal`/`dsep` 0.99 to
1.07 at 6 threads, 0.95 to 1.09 past 32 MB in the runs with and without
work per hop, single-threaded Part 1 noisier). One
translation instead of two saves at most the 12 to 14 ns of a page walk,
under 5 percent of a 250 ns hop.

**3. What does the table cost the marker? 1.8 to 1.9x, and L brings it to
1.6 to 1.7x.** A stop-the-world mark of the `walk` graph by one thread at
user-initiated QoS: phone 271 / 141 / 159 MB/s (A / B / L) at 128 MB live,
211 / 120 / 135 MB/s at 256 MB; Mac 244 / 122 / 126 and 235 / 117 / 122.
The marker does not pay the 4x of the single-threaded walk.

**4. Recommendation: accept B, without L.** See the last section.

So the device-measurement 7x (522 against 70 ns) is a fast-regime A against
a slow-regime B. The slow regime is a phone state reached by one
latency-bound thread on an idle P cluster; a table walk reaches it far more
often than a direct-pointer walk. Its cause inside the SoC is not
identified. The per-thread size dependence in device-measurement fits it:
with 3 or more workers the P cluster is never idle.

## Part 2: variant L

`swift/Sources/VML/` (`Blocks.swift` 189 lines, `Memory.swift` 659, against
B's 138 and 547), layout in SPEC.md: 16 KiB blocks (one page on both
machines), bodies up from the bottom, entries down from the top, one
compare on the allocation fast path; oop = entry offset from the heap base
as in B; `OM` keeps B's two base fields, both set to the heap base, so the
interpreter compiles to B's instructions; bodies over 4 KiB in a separate
non-moving arena of 32 KiB blocks with their entry in a small block.

Tests: object-representation's `VMTests.swift` runs on L unchanged except
its import block (copied to `Tests/VMLTests/`, compiled with `VARIANT_B`
so every B expectation applies): all 12 pass, plus 4 L tests (entry and
body share a page for 20 000 mixed allocations; a fresh block holds 409
40-byte objects; large bodies go to the large arena with a small entry;
walk after compaction equals walk after a non-moving GC). B's 12 tests pass
on the copy. `swift test --sanitize=thread`: 16 and 12 tests, no reports.
Checksums of every walk kernel on L equal A, B and C on both machines.

**Codegen.** The `push_ivar` handler (the body load of a hop) is the same
seven instructions in B and L, bases in two registers: `ldrb w8,[x24,#imm]`;
`ldr x9,[x22]` (receiver oop); `add x9,x9,x28` (table base, x20 in L);
`ldr x9,[x9,#imm]` (entry word 1: body offset); `add x9,x9,x20` (heap
base, x27 in L); `add x8,x9,x8,lsl #3`; `ldr x8,[x8,#imm]` (the slot). The send's class load
is `ldr` from the same entry. L's interpreter loop is 599 instructions
against B's 625; the difference is the allocation fast path and register
assignment, not the hop. The comparison is layout, not codegen.

## What L costs in complexity

- **Block size is now a correctness parameter.** 16 KiB is the only size
  that makes "same block" mean "same page", and it is half the shared heap's
  32 KiB, so `Big`'s 16 KiB chunk arrays no longer fit. L needed a second,
  large-object arena (its own blocks, pool, TLAB per worker, an epoch so a
  worker drops a large TLAB whose block a collection freed, and an
  operation TLAB for reshape) and a size threshold (4 KiB) that decides
  which objects get locality at all.
- **Sparse index space.** Entry index = block x 1024 + slot, most of which
  can never be an entry. Any side table indexed by entry (a mark byte map,
  ADR 0006's style) must cover `heap / 16` indices: 6.25 percent of the
  small arena reserved, and committed for every touched block whatever its
  entry count (1 KiB per block), against 1 byte per allocated entry in B
  (2.5 percent of B's bytes on `walk`). A bitmap would be 0.78 percent.
  Backrefs still fit 32 bits up to 64 GiB.
- **Entry runs per block.** B's per-worker runs, global run pool and
  high-water mark disappear (the block's gap is the run), which is simpler;
  but dead entries inside a block can only be reused when the whole block
  dies.
- **Entries pin blocks.** A live entry keeps its block in use after its
  body moved. Compaction therefore frees nothing when garbage is spread
  evenly (every block keeps live entries), and is useful only if the pool
  reuses the body gap of partially used blocks, which A and B never do.
  Compaction needs a pass to find each block's lowest live entry, must not
  read dead entries' slots once bodies have slid over them, and leaves
  every moved object non-local for good. The compaction move phase took
  1.06 (Mac) to 1.9 (phone) times B's.
- **Snapshot got simpler**, not harder: entries travel inside the heap
  blocks; there is no table and no free pool to rebuild on load. The block
  metadata grew by one word (`entryLow`).
- **Code:** +51 lines in the block arena, +112 in the object memory, all
  inside the two files; nothing outside the object memory knows.

## Recommendation for ADR 0003

**Accept B, the object table as it is; do not adopt L.** The ADR's two
conditions came out differently from how it framed them: locality does not
close the gap (L/B 0.91 to 1.11 everywhere), and the marker does not pay
4x (1.76 to 1.91x on the phone, 2.0x on the Mac). The 4 to 7x single-thread
walk is mostly a phone power regime, not cache or translation reach: with
the other P core busy the table costs 2.0 to 2.3x per cache-missing hop on
the phone, as on the Mac, and direct pointers fall into the same regime
(2.3 to 2.6x their fresh cost after a compaction). Heap offsets (C) would remove the table's 2x on
pointer chases and its 33 to 39 percent footprint, but bring back every
cost ADR 0003 accepted the table to avoid, for a cost that is now
understood and bounded. L buys 11 percent of marking on the phone and
nothing else, for a second arena, a sparse index space and a compaction
that cannot free blocks. Conditions:

1. The 2x per cache-missing hop and 1.8 to 1.9x marking are the accepted
   price; ADR 0006's old-space headroom must be sized for a marker at half
   a direct-pointer marker's rate (120 to 140 MB/s on one P core for a
   random 200 MB graph).
2. A latency-bound single thread on an idle P cluster can run 2.5 to 4
   times slower per miss on the phone for every representation, and more
   often with the table. The main thread running Smalltalk while workers
   idle is exactly that shape. Before the UI path relies on large
   traversals, measure a development-sized image's traversal on the main
   thread on device, alone and with a busy companion, and find out what the
   regime is (Instruments CPU Counters, or Apple's guidance) and whether a
   shell can avoid it cheaply.
3. Revisit C only if that measurement shows table walks in the slow regime
   dominating real UI work, or if footprint becomes the limit on device.

## Deviations and honest notes

- **Worker QoS.** object-representation's runner creates workers with
  default pthread attributes, which on Darwin give QoS `default`, not
  user-initiated (checked with a C probe and logged per run). This
  experiment uses a recorded copy, `swift/shared/Runner.swift`, symlinked
  into VMA, VMB, VMC and VML: workers at `KAJO_WORKER_QOS` (user-initiated
  unless set), the QoS each worker ran at in the line, and `RunResult`
  carries it. No other change to A, B or C; every other A/B/C file is a
  symlink to the original.
- **Kernels.** `lib.kasm` and object-representation's kernels are symlinked
  unchanged. The sized kernels use a 3-level `Big3` (in `el-lib.kasm`),
  because the 2-level `Big` needs a 4096-slot top array at 2^23 nodes, over
  the 32 KiB block limit. `walkcompact` kills one node in three, not one in
  two: with one dead per live node, 2^23 live nodes need more than B's
  fixed 16 Mi entries and B collected in the middle of the build.
  `KernelFiles.swift` is a recorded copy (kernel directory variable for the
  app, sized-kernel generation).
- **L design choices beyond the brief:** a large-object arena (bodies over
  4 KiB), partial-block reuse in L's pool, no single-base `OM`. The tests
  were written with the code, not strictly before it; all passed on the
  first run, which is weaker evidence than a red-green cycle.
- **Supplementary checks not in the brief:** CPU telemetry from
  `proc_pid_rusage`, the QoS check, independent work per hop, the
  companion thread (unthrottled and rate-capped), LCG order in the chase.
  Each was added after a result asked for it; each is a separate part of the
  scripts and a separate raw file.
- **Dropped or partial:** no performance counters (Instruments was not
  tried); Part 1's phone single-thread rows are medians of 3 launches over a
  bimodal distribution and move by up to 2x between runs; the mark was not
  run with a companion, so whether marks also hit the slow regime is open;
  the Mac ran while in interactive use (noisy single rows); the first phone
  `hop` matrix was aborted after 21 lines to add telemetry
  (`results/aborted/`); one `orig` launch (C walk) and two cool-down probes
  failed to launch and are recorded as `FAILED` lines; `results/device-*.txt`
  are the app's own copies pulled from Documents.
- The app has no spinning status view (device-measurement's had one) so
  the main thread does nothing during a run.
