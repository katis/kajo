# experiments

Throwaway spikes that answer a design question before it is locked in. One
directory per experiment with its own design brief and results. They stay in
the tree so ADRs can point at the evidence, and may be deleted once nothing
references them.

| Directory | Question | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `vm-language-bench/` | Rust or Swift for the VM core? | Swift. See `docs/adr/0001-swift-for-the-vm-core.md` |
| `object-representation/` | Direct pointers with forwarders, an object table, or heap offsets? | Object table, proposed. See `docs/adr/0003-object-representation.md` |
| `ui-thread-scheduling/` | Does the Smalltalk UI Process on the main thread, with budgeted preemption, Process-owned stacks, hop-to-main and GCD-signalled Semaphores, hold an AppKit window's refresh rate, and what does it cost? | Holds at 120 Hz except across GC pauses. Proposed in `docs/adr/0004-smalltalk-on-the-main-thread.md` |
| `generational-gc/` | Does a generational nursery on the object table VM keep young-collection pauses inside a 120 Hz frame while eight workers allocate, what does the write barrier cost, and does the main thread hold 120 Hz under the earlier allocation storm? | Nursery adopted; scavenges fit a frame for low survival, full collections still drop frames. Proposed in `docs/adr/0005-generational-collection.md` |
| `concurrent-marking/` | With snapshot-at-the-beginning concurrent marking and lazy sweeping of old space, do full collections become two short pauses, at what barrier and mutator cost, can one marker keep up with eight allocating workers, and does the main thread hold 120 Hz under the allocation storm and a large slowly mutating old heap? | Adopted. Final-mark pauses 0.1 ms flat from 64 to 512 MiB; needs old-space headroom or it falls back. Proposed in `docs/adr/0006-concurrent-marking.md` |
| `concurrent-relocation/` | Does an age field with survivor spaces cut the promotion rate and stop-the-world fallbacks under the allocation storm, can the marker thread relocate old-space bodies concurrently using the object table's entry as the single switch point plus a post-store check, what does that check cost, how much footprint does relocation reclaim, and does the main thread hold 120 Hz? | Age field N = 2 cut storm promotion 1490 to 1206 MB/s at 4 MiB nurseries (survivor overflow); 16 MiB nurseries cut it to 78 MB/s with 0 fallbacks. Relocation by the marker thread kept every checksum equal to stop-the-world in 45 runs per kernel; a 256 MiB mutating heap moved every cycle with 0 missed frames. Post-store check free on L1-hot stores, 5 to 13 percent on cache-missing ones. Footprint 87 MB against 230 MB headless, 92 against 392 MB in the app. Scavenges are now the frame-droppers. ADR 0007. |
| `concurrent-young/` | Can a worker's nursery be collected while every other worker keeps running (locally by the owner, or offloaded to a collector thread so the owner only flips), with what barrier and lost-parallelism cost, what must the initial mark do without a global scavenge, and does the main thread stop any more because another worker allocated? | Yes, and correct: checksums equal to stop-the-world everywhere, verifier and ThreadSanitizer clean. Main-thread time stopped for other workers' allocation under the storm fell 126 to 36 ms per 10 s (local) and 67 ms (offload); frames missed 58 to 5 or 6. Residue is round acknowledgements of about 30 us. Offload flips cost the main thread under 0.03 ms but one collector thread cannot serve eight workers. Initial mark now scans nurseries as roots, 2.4 ms at 8 workers. ADR 0008. |
